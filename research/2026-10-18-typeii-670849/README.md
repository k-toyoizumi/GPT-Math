## Known criterion and references
For prime p, the Type II divisor condition from Dahan (2026), Theorem 3.9, uses coprime positive u,a and a positive divisor s of a*p+u satisfying s ≡ -1 (mod 4*u*a). This is a **known** criterion, not our discovery.

- Benjamin Dahan, *Sieve dimension and search depth for the Erdős–Straus conjecture, n ≡ 1 (mod 24)*, arXiv:2608.24035, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24035

## Search completeness and computational conditions
For N>0, m=4ua, s>0 divides N and s ≡ -1 mod m iff s=mt-1 for integer 1≤t≤floor((N+1)/m), with N mod s=0. Proof: m|(s+1), positivity, and s≤N. The converse follows immediately. This elementary optimization reduces 91,235,600 naive candidates to 2,385,498 progression candidates (38.25-fold), not a new mathematical theorem.

Three exact standard-library Python methods cross-check: prime-factor divisor generation, trial divisors up to integer square root, and progression enumeration. Residue sets from prime powers are checked separately. No floating point or probabilistic tests. The Python audit does not independently prove primality of p; earlier project work checked it.
